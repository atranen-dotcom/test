# US Emergency & Urgent-Care Veterinary Industry and Competitive Landscape (as of Oct 2026)

Research date: 2026-10-19. About 18 searches/fetches. Many figures come from secondary or trade sources; quality caveats are noted inline. Items from prior knowledge that were NOT re-verified this session are flagged as such in Gaps.

## 1. Market size, growth, pet ownership/spending, pet insurance penetration

### Takeaway
US pet spending was about $158B in 2025 (APPA). "Vet care & product sales" was about $41B (+3% YoY), projected at about $42.4B in 2026. Growth is now low single digits and price-driven: visit volumes have fallen since 2022 while service prices keep rising. Pet insurance is growing about 9–20%/yr but covers only about 4.3% of US pets (NAPHIA, June 2026). The share of emergency/specialty is hard to size because IBISWorld's two figures conflict.

### Cited Findings
- 2025 total US pet industry spending was about $158B, +3.7% vs 2024. Vet care was the #2 category at 25.9% of spend, about $41.0B, +3% YoY (APPA 2026 State of the Industry) — [GlobalPETS](https://globalpetindustry.com/news/158b-us-pet-industry-maintains-steady-growth-through-2025/); [APPA press](https://www.accessnewswire.com/newsroom/en/business-and-professional-services/u.s.-pet-industry-reaches-158-billion-in-2025-poised-for-continu-1165595)
- APPA projects 2026 total spend near $165B and vet care & products at about $42.4B. APPA expects vet care to grow about 4%/yr through 2030. The $42.4B figure appears in secondary coverage only — [Pet Worldwide, Mar 2026](https://www.petworldwide.net/content-1/news/2026/03/31/plus/the-us-pet-food-industry-is-growing-steadily.html); [GlobalPETS](https://globalpetindustry.com/news/158b-us-pet-industry-maintains-steady-growth-through-2025/)
- Some secondary sources cite $39.8B for vet care. That is the 2024 figure — [World Animal Foundation](https://worldanimalfoundation.org/?p=22316)
- IBISWorld puts US Veterinary Services at $72.6B in 2026 (Jan 2026 report), with 57,920 businesses, growing at a 2.9% CAGR over 2021–26. Another IBISWorld page shows $74.5B (undated) — [IBISWorld Vet Services](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/market-research-reports/veterinary-services-industry/); [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/veterinary-services/1447/)
- IBISWorld's "Emergency Veterinary Services" figures CONFLICT. One page says $49.5B in 2025 (Sept 2025 report, +3.2%/yr over 5 years). Another says $2.3B in 2026 (Apr 2026 report, 1.4% 5-year CAGR, +2.4% expected in 2026). The $2.3B figure likely reflects a narrow standalone-ER scope, and the $49.5B figure is implausibly large (bigger than all of APPA vet care) — [IBISWorld EVS market size](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/market-size/emergency-veterinary-services/4025/); [IBISWorld EVS industry](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/emergency-veterinary-services-in-the-us/4025/)
- The Apr 2026 IBISWorld report cites Vetsource data showing visit volumes have declined four consecutive years since 2022 as the post-pandemic boost faded — [IBISWorld EVS](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/industry/emergency-veterinary-services-in-the-us/4025/)
- Vet visits fell 2.3% in 2024 vs 2023, and active patients fell 1.9% (Vetsource/VHMA). The average interval between visits rose from about 73 days (2020–21) to more than 112 days by mid-2024. Vet & grooming services CPI was +4.6% YoY (Apr 2025). Pet services prices are up 42% since 2019, vs 22% for food. In a Gallup-linked survey, 52% of owners skipped or declined care due to cost — [Octus, Jan 16 2026](https://octus.com/resources/articles/private-credit-exposure-to-veterinary-rollups-shows-growing-dispersion-vsos-under-increasing-pressure/)
- Pet insurance (NAPHIA 2026 SOI, released Jun 24 2026): about 7.6M pets insured in North America at end-2025, +8.5% from 7.03M. US insured pets grew +9%. US penetration is 4.27% overall (dogs 5.99%, cats 2.29%) — [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/breaking-news/naphia-only-4-27-of-us-pets-insured-despite-decade-of-doubledigit-growth-580314.aspx); [dvm360](https://www.dvm360.com/view/us-pet-insurance-enrollment-increased-9-in-2025-but-more-than-95-of-cats-and-dogs-remain-uninsured)
- US premium figures CONFLICT across sources: $5.2B (+20.8%, Insurance Business), about $5.68B (+19.7%, dvm360), and $6.2B (+19.4%, NAPHIA page, possibly North America-wide) — [Insurance Business](https://www.insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/breaking-news/naphia-only-4-27-of-us-pets-insured-despite-decade-of-doubledigit-growth-580314.aspx); [dvm360](https://www.dvm360.com/view/us-pet-insurance-enrollment-increased-9-in-2025-but-more-than-95-of-cats-and-dogs-remain-uninsured); [NAPHIA](https://naphia.org/industry-data/)
- Insured dog owners spend about $211/yr more on vet services (AVMA-funded study). Insurer reimbursement takes about 5–30+ days — [Octus](https://octus.com/resources/articles/private-credit-exposure-to-veterinary-rollups-shows-growing-dispersion-vsos-under-increasing-pressure/)

### Inferences
- Industry growth is now mostly price, not volume. ER is relatively insulated because emergencies are non-deferrable, unlike wellness visits. However, cost sensitivity (52% declining care) caps average invoice growth and raises "spectrum of care" and payment-plan pressure.
- Low insurance penetration means most ER bills are paid out of pocket. Insurance growth is a modest tailwind for high-ticket ER and specialty cases.

### Gaps
- No reliable standalone dollar figure for US emergency and specialty revenue. IBISWorld's numbers conflict, and the full reports are paywalled.
- APPA ownership counts (households owning pets, about 94M per the 2025 survey) and AVMA Pet Ownership & Demographics Sourcebook figures were not retrieved this session.

## 2. Major competitors and scale; who has struggled or closed (2024–2026)

### Takeaway
ER and specialty is heavily corporatized: about 75% of specialty and emergency clinics are corporate-owned, vs 25–50% of general practices. Mars (BluePearl, about 110 ER/specialty hospitals, plus VCA) is the largest operator. Other major ER/specialty operators are Ethos (spun from NVA, about 145 specialty hospitals at its 2023 formation), Thrive/Pathway, PetVet, MedVet, and SAGE. Leveraged PE roll-ups show clear credit stress: Thrive did a 2025 distressed exchange and was later downgraded by Moody's, and PetVet, AmeriVet, and UVC had their marks cut. Urgent-care and "modern GP" chains are consolidating, with Bond Vet and Small Door merging in July 2026.

### Cited Findings
- About 75% of specialty and emergency clinics are corporate-owned, vs 25–50% of GPs. PE invested an estimated $45B in US vet deals from 2017–2022 (PitchBook) — [Octus, Jan 2026](https://octus.com/resources/articles/private-credit-exposure-to-veterinary-rollups-shows-growing-dispersion-vsos-under-increasing-pressure/)
- **BluePearl (Mars):** "more than 110 hospitals in 30 states" (Mars release). A Sept 2025 release says "over 100." BluePearl has also opened a first stand-alone urgent care hospital — [Mars](https://www.mars.com/news-and-stories/press-releases-statements/bluepearl-announces-new-hospital-monroeville); [BluePearl Franklin release](https://bluepearlvet.com/press-releases-announcements/bluepearl-franklin-relocating-to-expanded-location/); [BluePearl urgent care](https://bluepearlvet.com/press-releases-announcements/bluepearl-opens-first-ever-stand-alone-urgent-care-hospital/)
- **Mars overall:** more than 3,000 clinics worldwide — [Octus](https://octus.com/resources/articles/private-credit-exposure-to-veterinary-rollups-shows-growing-dispersion-vsos-under-increasing-pressure/). Mars Veterinary Health family includes VCA, Banfield, BluePearl, and others — [Mars Veterinary Health](https://marsveterinary.com/who-we-are/our-companies/)
- **NVA / Ethos:** Ethos Veterinary Health was formed in 2023 as a separate specialty/ER business with 145 specialty hospitals. NVA kept about 1,400 mostly GP locations. A 2026 total was not disclosed — [Ethos](https://www.ethosvet.com/news-article/new-hospital-network-known-as-ethos-veterinary-health/); [Ethos/NVA](https://www.ethosvet.com/news-article/nva-and-ethos-veterinary-health/)
- **Thrive Pet Healthcare (fka Pathway Vet Alliance; TSG Consumer):** closed a financing on Mar 31 2025 adding more than $350M of liquidity and extending maturities on more than $1.7B of debt to June 2028. Octus and S&P describe it as a liability management exercise / distressed exchange. Causes cited: higher labor costs, staffing shortages, and volume declines — [Thrive release](https://www.thrivepetcare.com/thrive-guide/thrive-pet-healthcare-secures-new-financing); [Ropes & Gray](https://ropesgray.com/en/news-and-events/news/2025/03/ropes-gray-advises-thrive-pet-healthcare-on-liability-management-transaction); [Octus](https://octus.com/resources/articles/private-credit-exposure-to-veterinary-rollups-shows-growing-dispersion-vsos-under-increasing-pressure/)

Later reporting says Moody's downgraded Thrive on high default probability even though adjusted EBITDA and revenue rose YoY in Q2 2026. Lenders are again engaging Akin Gump and PJT ahead of the 2028 maturity wall. Dates in the Octus snippets are imprecise — [Octus](https://octus.com/?p=45673); [Octus pro rata](https://octus.com/resources/articles/thrive-pet-negotiating-pro-rata-deal-with-lenders-for-new-money)
- **PetVet Care Centers (KKR):** 450+ hospitals. Ares marked its loan at 88% of par (Q3 2025), the lowest BDC mark in the sector. KKR cited an "unfavorable business outlook" — [Octus](https://octus.com/resources/articles/private-credit-exposure-to-veterinary-rollups-shows-growing-dispersion-vsos-under-increasing-pressure/)
- **United Veterinary Care (Nordic):** 100+ hospitals. Its first lien is marked at 95.7% and it moved from all-cash pay to partial PIK. **AmeriVet (AEA):** subordinated loan marked at 83%; it has 186 clinics — [Octus](https://octus.com/resources/articles/private-credit-exposure-to-veterinary-rollups-shows-growing-dispersion-vsos-under-increasing-pressure/)
- **Mission Pet Health (fka Southern Veterinary Partners + Mission Veterinary Partners; Shore Capital + Silver Lake):** valued at $8.6B, with about $580M EBITDA (about 14.8x) and 750+ locations. It has a $2.893B first lien term loan due 2031, repriced Jul 2025 — [Octus](https://octus.com/resources/articles/private-credit-exposure-to-veterinary-rollups-shows-growing-dispersion-vsos-under-increasing-pressure/). In 2026, Four Corners Property Trust agreed to buy up to 102 Mission properties for up to $268M (sale-leaseback, expected close early Q3 2026) — [FCPT 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001650132/000119312526250037/fcpt-ex99_1.htm); [Pet Age](https://www.petage.com/fcpt-agrees-to-acquire-up-to-102-mission-pet-health-veterinary-properties/). Location counts vary from 840+ to 930+ in 2026 (single blog source) — [Transitions Elite](https://transitionselite.com/who-owns-mission-pet-health-private-equity-owner/)
- **Western Veterinary Partners:** continuation vehicle at $2B EV, a "high-teens EBITDA multiple" — [Octus](https://octus.com/resources/articles/private-credit-exposure-to-veterinary-rollups-shows-growing-dispersion-vsos-under-increasing-pressure/)
- **Bond Vet + Small Door:** merger finalized Jul 9 2026, creating more than 55 clinics and 500K+ pets across the Northeast, Mid-Atlantic, and Midwest, led by Florent Peyre. Brands run separately for now. Bond Vet is an urgent-care/GP hybrid, and Small Door is membership primary care — [dvm360](https://www.dvm360.com/view/bond-vet-and-small-door-veterinary-merge-to-form-55-clinic-network); [PR Newswire via Morningstar](https://www.morningstar.com/news/pr-newswire/20260709la00717/bond-vet-and-small-door-veterinary-merge-to-expand-access-to-trusted-veterinary-care). Bond Vet Rye Brook, NY was closed (Jul 2026) per a PE-tracking database — [Private Equity Vet changelog](https://privateequityvet.org/changelog/)
- **Modern Animal:** raised a $46M Series D in Sept 2025. It has about 27 clinics in CA, TX, and CO (Feb 2026 report). One secondary source says it closed North Austin in Jul 2025 and consolidated members into other Austin sites; this is unverified — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/09/16/exclusive-modern-animal-veterinary-clinic-network-raises-46-million-series-d); [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/modern-animal)
- **Heart + Paw:** about 28–29 locations, using a co-ownership de novo model (blog source) — [Transitions Elite](https://transitionselite.com/emerging-veterinary-consolidators-2026/)
- Chewy Vet Care is expanding, including a first Pacific Northwest site in Redmond, WA (2026) — [dvm360](https://www.dvm360.com/view/clinic-center-chewy-vet-care-opens-first-pacific-northwest-location-in-redmond-washington-small-door-veterinary-expands-to-cambridge-following-bond-vet-merger-and-more)

### Inferences
- Leveraged GP-heavy roll-ups (VSOs) are under the most stress: falling wellness volume plus labor inflation plus floating-rate debt. Specialty/ER credits were marked at or above par (100.8% avg for "specialty" in BDC books), which suggests ER/specialty economics have held up better.
- Consolidation among VC-backed urgent-care and "modern GP" chains (Bond + Small Door) indicates subscale de novo models struggled to reach standalone profitability.

### Gaps
- Current 2026 hospital counts were not retrieved for VCA (historically about 1,000+ US hospitals, of which a few dozen offer 24-hr ER), MedVet (historically about 40+), SAGE, Thrive (historically about 380+), or independent ERs. Pet Urgent Care franchises and Vetco/Petco (Petco 10-Q exists but was not read) were not covered.
- Thrive's exact Moody's downgrade date and rating level were not confirmed.

## 3. Veterinarian shortage, ER DVM/tech supply, compensation, burnout, closures/reduced hours

### Takeaway
Whether there is an overall DVM shortage is disputed. The AVMA-commissioned Brakke work says supply is adequate to 2035, while Mars/NAVEC/AAVMC see large gaps. The ER-specific and overnight shortage, especially technicians, is well documented in local reporting through 2026. Asking pay for ER DVMs in 2026 postings is about $150K–$325K base for roughly 3×12-hr shifts/week. Hospitals keep cutting overnight hours or turning patients away.

### Cited Findings
- 2026 ER DVM job postings: Texas 24/7 ER at $190K–$325K (no production quota, posted Mar 29 2026); San Antonio at $170K–$200K base for 30–40 hrs of swing/overnight; Michigan at $150K–$200K base for 3×12-hr shifts; NY specialty at a guaranteed $200K for max 3 shifts/week; St. Louis at a $150K guaranteed minimum (typical about $200K) with overnight differentials — [iHire](https://ihireveterinary.com/jobs/view/466087575); [iHire](https://ihire.com/jobs/view/472999184); [Workable](https://jobs.workable.com/jobs/86036fdb-b90d-480f-a497-410ee48c5674.md)
- AVMA (2019, dated): the ER doctor shortage was "critical," worst in small towns that cannot match the $150K pay of metro specialty hospitals. Some older DVMs were working 60-hr overnight weeks — [AVMA JAVMA News](https://www.avma.org/News/JAVMANews/Pages/190301a.aspx)
- AAVMC study: only about 76% of US veterinary demand will be met by 2032 — [Octus](https://octus.com/resources/articles/private-credit-exposure-to-veterinary-rollups-shows-growing-dispersion-vsos-under-increasing-pressure/); [AAVMC PDF](https://www.aavmc.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Demand-for-and-Supply-of-Veterinarians-in-the-U.S.-to-2032-New.pdf)
- AVMA-commissioned Brakke forecast: educational capacity is sufficient to meet demand to 2035, and supply may outpace demand after 2035 — [AVMA](https://www.avma.org/news/no-dire-shortage-veterinarians-anticipated-coming-years). Mars estimates up to 55,000 more DVMs needed by 2030. NAVEC (advocacy) estimates a 37,000 FTE shortfall in 2026, rising to 83,000 by 2031 — [AVMA](https://www.avma.org/blog/chart-month-no-dire-shortage-veterinarians-ahead); [NAVEC](https://navec.org/research.html)
- Technicians: in NAVTA's 2024 survey, only 36% felt fully utilized. BLS projects +9% tech employment growth 2024–34. Average vacancy durations of 12.8 months for credentialed techs and 15.4 for DVMs are attributed to AAHA via a vendor blog (unverified) — [Boostpoint](https://boostpoint.com/veterinary-staffing-shortage/); [VetBridge 2026](https://www.vetbridgeusa.com/reports/state-of-the-veterinary-workforce-2026)
- Burnout: the AVMA 2024 census average ProQOL burnout score was 26.4/50 (moderate), with early-career DVMs at 28.1. In AVMA 2025 data, among those considering leaving, 33.7% cite mental health and 16.6% cite lifestyle/hours — [Digitail summary](https://digitail.com/blog/10-veterinary-stats-and-findings-2026/)
- Reduced hours and closures, 2026 local reports:
  - University of Minnesota Lewis Small Animal Hospital closes midnight–8am Wed–Sat due to an overnight tech shortage — [Yahoo/News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/us/articles/u-m-vet-clinic-reduces-204552817.html)
  - Richmond SPCA hospital cut its hours (May 2026) — [12 On Your Side](https://www.12onyourside.com/2026/05/06/richmond-spca-adjusts-veterinary-hospital-hours-due-staffing-shortage/)
  - Monroe County, NY's only 24/7 ER (REVS) is often at capacity and turning away pets — [Rochester First](https://www.rochesterfirst.com/news/no-quick-fixes-monroe-countys-only-24-7-emergency-vet-short-staffed/)
  - Santa Barbara County lost a 24/7 ER and now has two — [Noozhawk](https://www.noozhawk.com/veterinary-staff-burnout-a-crisis-beyond-santa-barbara-county/)
  - Columbia, MO (2024): both hospitals limited overnight hours — [KOMU](https://www.komu.com/news/midmissourinews/columbia-animal-hospitals-limit-overnight-hours-due-to-staffing-shortages/article_4144f952-8bd4-11ef-8e49-ab07aec27c8a.html)
  - A broader trend of hospitals suspending 24-hr service — [Whole Dog Journal](https://www.whole-dog-journal.com/blog/many-veterinary-hospitals-are-suspending-24-hour-emergency-service/)

### Inferences
- The binding constraint for ER operators is overnight staffing (DVM and techs), not demand. A model that makes ER shifts more attractive (pay, schedule, no production pressure, culture) is a key competitive moat.
- Salary inflation for ER DVMs (bases of $150K–$325K vs about $150K in 2019 metro postings) materially raises the fixed cost of a 24/7 hospital.

### Gaps
- No national survey of ER DVM compensation. VIN or AVMA ER-specific comp data was not found, so posted ranges are asking prices, not averages. Per-shift relief rates (commonly cited informally at about $1,500–$2,500+/shift) were not sourced.
- No national tally of ER closures or reduced hours for 2025–26.

## 4. PE consolidation, valuations, recent deals, regulatory scrutiny (FTC/CMA)

### Takeaway
Specialty and ER assets command premium multiples. Private ER clinics sit at about 10x EBITDA in the $5–10M band and specialists at about 13x. Scaled platforms trade in the mid-to-high teens: Mission at about 14.8x, Western Vet at high teens. Small private practices trade at mid-to-high single digits. US antitrust action has been merger-specific: the FTC's 2017 Mars/VCA consent required divesting 12 specialty/ER clinics. The UK CMA issued final remedies on Mar 24 2026 (price transparency, ownership disclosure, prescription caps). These could become a template for US pricing scrutiny.

### Cited Findings
- First Page Sage (Q1 2025), $5–10M EBITDA band: veterinary specialists about 13.2x, emergency clinics about 10.4x, general practice about 11.3x — [First Page Sage](https://firstpagesage.com/business/veterinary-practice-ebitda-valuation-multiples/)
- Private practice multiples were mid-to-high single digits as of Q1 2025, with specialists higher — [Octus](https://octus.com/resources/articles/private-credit-exposure-to-veterinary-rollups-shows-growing-dispersion-vsos-under-increasing-pressure/)
- Broker claims (less reliable): specialty and ER deals at 15–22x; 10–12x+ for specialty/ER above $2M EBITDA; a specialty/ER premium of 1–2.5 turns over GP; roll-up arbitrage of buying at 5–7x and exiting at 12–15x — [Transitions Elite](https://transitionselite.com/ebitda-benchmarks-in-vet-practice-sales/); [Ad Astra](https://www.adastraequity.com/ebitda-multiples/veterinary-practice); [Sofer Advisors](https://soferadvisors.com/insights/blog/veterinary-practice-valuation-methods-and-multiples/)
- Platform marks: Mission Pet Health at $8.6B on about $580M EBITDA; Western Veterinary Partners at $2B EV, high-teens multiple; BDCs held $3.1B of vet loans across 15 companies (Q3 2025) — [Octus](https://octus.com/resources/articles/private-credit-exposure-to-veterinary-rollups-shows-growing-dispersion-vsos-under-increasing-pressure/)
- Recent deals (2025–26):
  - AmeriVet acquired 14 Northeast Veterinary Partners practices (Nov 2025) — [Octus](https://octus.com/resources/articles/private-credit-exposure-to-veterinary-rollups-shows-growing-dispersion-vsos-under-increasing-pressure/)
  - Bond Vet + Small Door merger (Jul 2026) — [dvm360](https://www.dvm360.com/view/bond-vet-and-small-door-veterinary-merge-to-form-55-clinic-network)
  - FCPT–Mission sale-leaseback ($268M, 102 properties) — [FCPT 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001650132/000119312526250037/fcpt-ex99_1.htm)
  - Running M&A list — [GlobalPETS](https://globalpetindustry.com/news/latest-ma-deals-in-veterinary-care-diagnostics-and-pet-tech/)
- FTC Mars/VCA (File 171-0057, 2017; $9.1B deal):
  - Mars divested 12 specialty/ER clinics in 10 local markets to NVA, Pathway, or PetVet.
  - Mars may not contract with specialty/ER DVMs affiliated with the divested clinics for 1 year.
  - Mars must give 10-year prior notice of specialty/ER acquisitions in certain areas.
  - The order was modified after public comment.
  - [FTC analysis](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/cases/171_0057_mars-vca_analysis.pdf); [FTC 2017 release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2017/12/ftc-alters-final-consent-order-response-public-comments-preserving-competition-specialty-emergency); [dvm360](https://dvm360.com/view/ftc-requires-mars-divest-12-veterinary-clinics-nationwide)
- FTC: Compassion First and NVA agreed to divest facilities in three locations to MedVet (FTC listing) — [FTC OIG listing](https://oig.ftc.gov/industry/veterinarians?page=1)
- UK CMA final report (Mar 24 2026) found "significant and widespread problems": poor information, market barriers, and weak regulation. Remedies:
  - Mandatory comprehensive online price lists
  - Disclosure of group ownership
  - Written prescriptions
  - Prescription fee cap raised to £21 for the first item and £12.50 for each additional item
  - Promotion of online pharmacies from June 2027
  - Implementation via CMA Order/RCVS by about Sept 23 2026
  - [VBB](https://vbb.com/articles/uk-cma-concludes-veterinary-sector-market-investigation-and-announces-major-reforms/); [GOV.UK guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/what-veterinary-businesses-and-vets-need-to-do-following-the-cmas-final-vets-report); [Concurrences](https://www.concurrences.com/en/bulletin/news-issues/march-2026/the-uk-competition-authority-imposes-wide-ranging-remedies-following-a-market); [Agilio](https://agiliosoftware.com/veterinary/insights/cma-final-decision/)

### Inferences
- An ER/specialty platform with strong growth could command low-to-high teens EBITDA multiples. The spread between ER-clinic (about 10x) and platform (about 15x+) multiples supports continued roll-up and de novo strategies, but leverage discipline matters given Thrive/PetVet stress.
- The US equivalent of CMA price-transparency rules does not exist yet. ER pricing (the largest out-of-pocket bills) is the most exposed to future state or federal transparency moves.

### Gaps
- From prior knowledge, NOT re-verified this session: the FTC's 2022 consent order on JAB Consumer Partners (NVA owner) acquiring SAGE Veterinary Centers and Ethos. It reportedly required divestitures of ER/specialty clinics in California and Texas and imposed 10-year prior approval/notice provisions for nearby vet acquisitions. Confirm on ftc.gov before citing.
- No US FTC vet-pricing action in 2024–26 was found. No PitchBook/Axios Pro 2025–26 vet deal volume data was retrieved, and no Simmons appraiser multiples.

## 5. Emergency hospital unit economics

### Takeaway
Sourcing is thin and mostly contractor or consultant marketing. A full ER/specialty build costs roughly $2.5–5M+ for 8,000–15,000+ SF at $400–700/SF, plus $300K–$1M of equipment. ER DVMs generate about $1–1.5M+ in revenue each. ER margins are cited at 15–25%, vs 10–15% for GP. The median full ER invoice is about $780–810, and the exam fee alone is about $135–143.

### Cited Findings
- Construction: ER/specialty referral hospitals with CT/MRI and 24-hr ICU run $400–$700/SF. An 8,000–15,000+ SF ER/specialty hospital costs $2.5–$5M+. General vet clinics run $225–$350/SF — [Terrapin CG](https://terrapincg.com/news/average-cost-to-build-veterinary-clinic-usa)
- Equipment adds $300K–$1M depending on diagnostic and critical care scope. A GP startup example totals about $433K of startup assets (digital X-ray $75K, ultrasound $60K, surgical $40K) — [Nidana 2026](https://nidana.io/blog/cost-to-open-veterinary-clinic); [ProjectionHub](https://www.projectionhub.com/post/opening-a-profitable-vet-clinic-numbers-you-need-to-know)
- Revenue: AVMA average practice (all types) has about $1.9M revenue and 12.4K visits (via vendor blog). ER and board-certified specialists generate $1M–$1.5M+/yr per DVM — [Maven Imaging](https://www.mavenimaging.com/blog/veterinary-clinic-revenue-breakdown); [Transitions Elite](https://transitionselite.com/vet-practice-owner-salary/)
- Margins: 15–25% for emergency/specialty vs 10–15% for small-animal GP (secondary) — [Transitions Elite](https://transitionselite.com/vet-practice-owner-salary/)
- ER exam fee (CareCredit 2025): dogs $135 (range $107–246), cats $143 (range $113–260). Exam plus X-rays plus basic bloodwork is about $680–740 — [CareCredit](https://www.carecredit.com/well-u/pet-care/emergency-vet-visit-cost-and-veterinary-financing/)
- Whole ER visit: median $808 (typical $409–$1,807, from 400+ invoices). Another source gives a $777 median with a $411–$1,433 range — [VetReceipt](https://vetreceipt.com/vet-care-cost); [Webvet](https://www.webvet.com/emergency-vet-cost/); BluePearl's guidance on bill components — [BluePearl](https://bluepearlvet.com/pet-blog/pet-emergency-bill/)
- COGS benchmarks for vet hospitals — [dvm360](https://www.dvm360.com/view/understanding-cogs-in-a-veterinary-hospital)

### Inferences
- Illustrative 24/7 ER: at about 25–40 cases/day and about $800 average invoice, annual revenue would be about $7–12M. This is an inference, not sourced. A 24/7 schedule needs about 8–12 DVM FTEs at 3×12-hr shifts and about 3–4 techs per DVM on shift. At about $200K–$250K fully loaded per DVM, DVM labor alone is about $2–3M.
- Breakeven is likely 2–4 years for a de novo ER given ramp and fixed overnight staffing. No source was found.

### Gaps
- No credible source for ER-specific revenue per hospital, EBITDA margin distribution, breakeven timelines, or staffing ratios (DVM:tech) was found. VHMA/AAHA or Brakke ER benchmarks are paywalled or were not located.

## 6. Underserved geography: ER "deserts" and wait times

### Takeaway
Evidence is mostly local and anecdotal but consistent. Rural and exurban areas face 1–3 hour drives to the nearest ER, and the remaining ERs are frequently at capacity with multi-hour waits or diversion. No national drive-time or wait-time dataset was found.

### Cited Findings
- Door County, WI (Aug 2026): the nearest ER is 1–3 hours away. A Sturgeon Bay clinic gets 5+ emergency calls a day it cannot fully serve. Wisconsin's limited vet training capacity is cited — [Wisconsin Watch](https://wisconsinwatch.org/2026/08/wisconsin-door-county-veterinarian-shortage-clinics-dog-cat-pet-owners-emergency-care/); [Open Campus](https://www.opencampus.org/2026/08/20/vet-shortage-overwhelms-clinics-leaves-owners-to-drive-hours-for-emergency-care/)
- An owner was told the ER was at capacity with a wait of "several more hours" (republished account) — [MyVetCandy](https://www.myvetcandy.com/news/2026/8/20/when-the-nearest-emergency-vet-is-90-minutes-away-what-happens-to-the-pets-who-cant-wait)
- JAVMA secret-shopper study (Nov 2025, about 5,000 practices in 6 states, routine care): rural practices booked appointments less than 62% of the time, with an average wait of about 9 days and 13 miles of travel — [Ohio State/JAVMA via syndication](https://tremontpharmacyoh.com/patient-resources/article/1762362554698/rural-pet-owners-have-a-tougher-time-seeing-a-vet-study-says)
- Monroe County, NY's only 24/7 ER regularly cannot take new patients — [Rochester First](https://www.rochesterfirst.com/news/no-quick-fixes-monroe-countys-only-24-7-emergency-vet-short-staffed/). Santa Barbara County is down to two 24/7 ERs — [Noozhawk](https://www.noozhawk.com/veterinary-staff-burnout-a-crisis-beyond-santa-barbara-county/)
- A new 24-hr pet ER in Petaluma, CA (Aug 2026) was framed as filling a gap amid exploding demand — [Hoodline](https://hoodline.com/2026/08/petaluma-s-new-24-hour-pet-er-fills-a-gap-as-vet-demand-explodes/)

### Inferences
- White space exists both in rural and exurban areas (long drives) and in suburban metros, where incumbent ERs are capacity-constrained by staffing (long waits, diversion). For a well-staffed operator, the second may be the more economically attractive gap.

### Gaps
- No national geospatial analysis of ER drive times and no systematic ER wait-time data (e.g., median door-to-doctor time) was found.
