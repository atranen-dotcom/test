# Veterinary Emergency Group (VEG, now branded "VEG ER for Pets"): Company Profile (as of Oct 19, 2026)

Research note: the research used about 19 web searches and fetches. Business Wire, PitchBook, Glassdoor and Westfair returned 403 errors or bot walls. Business Wire release facts below come from search-result snippets of that release, not from a full-text read. Every company figure is labeled with its date. Most operating figures (revenue, headcount) are company-sourced statements from 2023–2025, not audited numbers.

## 1. History, founders, leadership, HQ

### Takeaway
VEG was founded in 2014 by emergency veterinarian Dr. David Bessler, VMD. He bought one ER hospital in White Plains, NY. David Glattstein, a former PE/investment professional, is co-founder and president and drove capital raising and scale-up. Bessler is still Founder/CEO as of the latest sources (2026). The company has rebranded from "Veterinary Emergency Group" to "VEG ER for Pets."

## 2. Business model specifics

### Takeaway
VEG is a walk-in ER for pets: emergency and urgent care only, 24/7/365, no appointments. Its signature is an "open-concept" hospital where owners stay with the pet through treatment, including surgery and overnight stays. Callers are connected to a licensed veterinarian by phone. VEG is not a specialty/referral hospital model. The service list covers emergency surgery, endoscopy, imaging, transfusions and hospitalization.

### Cited Findings
- Open floor plan: exam tables, computers and desks in the middle; kennels on the perimeter; surgery, X-ray and private exam rooms at the back. Every facility has at least one private exam room, and some use privacy screens. As of Oct 2022, VEG had 32 hospitals in 11 states and about 2,000 staff. Bessler said only "three or four people out of 2,000" tried the model and didn't like it. — [Today's Veterinary Business, Oct 2022](https://todaysveterinarybusiness.com/pets-open-concept-veterinary-hospitals/)
- 2026 model description:
  - Callers are connected right away with a licensed veterinarian.
  - Owners can stay through all stages, including surgery and overnight stays.
  - Triage begins on arrival without waiting for intake forms.
  - Open 24/7 including holidays.
  - Services: surgery, endoscopy, ultrasound and X-ray, diagnostics, hospitalization.
  - Species treated: dogs, cats, small mammals, birds, nonvenomous reptiles and turtles.
  - Source: [Pembroke Pines FL News, Jul 17 2026](https://www.pembrokepinesflnews.com/news/local/article316548391.html)
- Etobicoke service list: emergency and urgent care, hospitalization, surgery, blood transfusions, X-rays, ultrasounds, endoscopy. Accepts all animals except venomous species and primates. — [Retail Insider, Feb 2026](https://retail-insider.com/retail-insider/2026/02/veg-er-for-pets-opens-etobicoke-emergency-hospital-as-part-of-north-american-expansion/)
- "Walk-in ER for pets open 24/7." The model emphasizes transparency and involving pet parents in treatment (sponsored content). — [dvm360, Aug 30 2023](https://www.dvm360.com/view/veg-to-open-15-more-locations-in-2023)
- Payments and insurance: in a Dec 2022 Q&A, Bessler opposed installing Trupanion's direct-pay/pre-approval software in VEG hospitals. He cited fear that insurance would "ruin the veterinary industry" as in human medicine. Asked about clients who can't pay upfront, he said "I don't have a solution for that." The source is critical/opinion-leaning. — [The Canine Review, Jan 30 2023](https://thecaninereview.com/2023/01/30/veterinary-emergency-group-ceo-attacks-pet-insurers-offers-no-solution-for-pet-parents-who-cant-pay-vet-bills-up-front/)
- Philanthropy arm "VEG Cares": a donation fund for pets in need, plus scholarships for aspiring vets. — [Retail Insider](https://retail-insider.com/retail-insider/2026/02/veg-er-for-pets-opens-etobicoke-emergency-hospital-as-part-of-north-american-expansion/); [dvm360 2023](https://www.dvm360.com/view/veg-to-open-15-more-locations-in-2023). VEG reportedly raised over $1M for charity. — [dvm360](https://dvm360.com/view/veterinary-emergency-group-raises-over-1-million-for-charity)
- New initiative (2026): Maddie's Fund lists VEG (San Ramon, CA) as a new grant partner running a pilot to expand access to surgical care. Listed total is $750,000 "since 2026." This is grant money, not equity. — [Maddie's Fund](https://www.maddiesfund.org/veg.htm)

### Inferences
- The free "talk to a vet" phone line works as triage and customer acquisition. It substitutes for a formal telehealth product. I found no evidence of a VEG consumer app or a paid telehealth service.
- The Maddie's Fund surgical-access pilot may signal a move toward lower-cost or "spectrum of care" surgery. That is speculative.

### Gaps
- No public detail on a "VEG app," formal telehealth or any specialty/referral service line. The searches did not confirm any.
- Published price lists or a formal pricing-transparency policy: none found. "Transparency" in sources refers to visibility of care, not posted prices.

## 4. Funding history, valuation, debt

### Takeaway
VEG has three verified equity rounds. Sequoia Heritage led or funded rounds 1 and 2, starting in 2017. D1 Capital Partners and Fidelity co-led a $100M third round in Sept 2021, with Durable Capital Partners participating. Total equity was about $150M per the company at the time. No verified equity round since 2021. PitchBook shows the latest deal type as Debt. No reliably reported valuation.

### Cited Findings
- Sept 22 2021: $100M third round, co-led by D1 Capital Partners and Fidelity Management & Research Company LLC, supported by Durable Capital Partners LP. Brings total equity raised since inception to about $150M. Use of proceeds: recruitment, school-debt repayment, credential support, nursing salaries, continuing education, well-being. — [Business Wire, Sep 22 2021](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210922005550/en/) (snippet-level; full text blocked)
- Sequoia Heritage invested "millions" in VEG's first two rounds, starting 2017. — same Business Wire release (via search summary); Sequoia Heritage named as backer — [Emory Business 2023](https://www.emorybusiness.com/2023/08/09/meet-david-glattstein-changing-the-game-for-veterinary-emergency-care/)
- PitchBook: latest deal type "Debt"; total funding $111M (database figure, conflicts with the ~$150M company figure). — [PitchBook (search snippet)](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/252217-72)
- Unverified claim: a SEO-style blog says VEG raised a $500M round in 2024 at a $2.5B+ valuation and gives a ~$2.8B 2025 valuation. No named source and no press release corroborates it, so it should not be relied on. — [businessmodelcanvastemplate.com](https://businessmodelcanvastemplate.com/blogs/owners/veterinary-emergency-group-who-owns)

### Inferences
- The brief suggested Brighton Park and Ares as investors. I found no evidence for either; the verified investor list is Sequoia Heritage, D1, Fidelity and Durable.
- Going from ~50 to 130+ sites after 2021 with no announced equity suggests funding from operating cash flow plus undisclosed debt and/or unannounced private equity. That is consistent with the PitchBook "Debt" tag.

### Gaps
- Debt facility size, lender and date are not found.
- No credible valuation reported.
- Any 2022–2026 equity raise is unconfirmed. Crunchbase and CB Insights pages were not retrievable.

## 5. Revenue, visits, employees, unit economics

### Takeaway
Company-sourced figures: revenue above $500M (Aug 2023, at 53 hospitals); employees about 2,000 (Oct 2022), 2,600 (Dec 2022), 3,500+ (Aug 2023), 5,500 (~Mar 2025); 1M+ pets and owners helped cumulatively (2023). Unit economics and average ticket are not disclosed.

### Cited Findings
- Revenue "exceeds $500 million" annually, 3,500+ employees ("VEGgies"), 1M+ pets and owners helped since founding. Company-supplied figures in an alumni-magazine profile. — [Emory Business, Aug 9 2023](https://www.emorybusiness.com/2023/08/09/meet-david-glattstein-changing-the-game-for-veterinary-emergency-care/)
- About 2,000 staff (Oct 2022) — [Today's Veterinary Business](https://todaysveterinarybusiness.com/pets-open-concept-veterinary-hospitals/); about 2,600 (Jan 2023) — [Canine Review](https://thecaninereview.com/2023/01/30/veterinary-emergency-group-ceo-attacks-pet-insurers-offers-no-solution-for-pet-parents-who-cant-pay-vet-bills-up-front/); 5,500 (~2025) — [Patch](https://patch.com/new-york/new-york-city/small-door-veterinary-appoints-first-ever-chief-operating-officer)

### Inferences (estimates, not reported)
- $500M+ across 53 hospitals (many newly opened) would imply about $9–10M+ per hospital. That is very high for a ~5,000 sq ft ER. Possible explanations: the figure is a run rate, it counts system-wide gross charges, or it is inflated in an alumni-magazine context. Treat it with caution.
- Staff per hospital is roughly 55–65, using 3,500/53 (2023) and 5,500/100 (2025).
- Extrapolating ~$5–10M per site to 130 sites gives a rough 2026 revenue range of about $650M–$1.3B. This is a pure estimate.

### Gaps

## 6. Pricing, reputation, culture, compensation, training

### Takeaway
VEG reports very high customer scores: Google 4.7 average and NPS 91 (2023, company-reported). Criticism centers on upfront payment, refusal of insurer direct-pay, and isolated malpractice and employment lawsuits. Culture is branded heavily ("VEGgies"; openness, togetherness, heroic help). Funding was earmarked for school-debt repayment and nurse pay. Training runs mainly through vet-student externships and scribe internships. No formal residency was found.
